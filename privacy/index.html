MsgCraft Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 4, 2026
Developer: MsgCraft developer (an individual)
Contact: msgcraft1@gmail.com

MsgCraft ("the app") is an unofficial app for reading and sending chat on Minecraft: Java Edition servers.
NOT AN OFFICIAL MINECRAFT PRODUCT. NOT APPROVED BY OR ASSOCIATED WITH MOJANG OR MICROSOFT.

Summary

  • The developer runs no servers, and the app sends the developer nothing. If you buy a paid item, the developer can see only the sales totals and order information that Apple or Google shows in its store console (see 2(5)). If you send a report e-mail yourself, the developer receives that e-mail (see 2(6)).
  • The app stores its data only on your device (it is not included in Android's Google Drive backup; on iPhone it can be part of your own iCloud or computer backup of the device).
  • The app connects directly to the Minecraft servers you choose and any server they move you to (see 2(1)), to the Microsoft, Xbox and Mojang services needed for sign-in, to Mojang's file servers for language files, to Cloudflare or Google to look up server addresses, and to the App Store or Google Play. Some of these connections happen automatically, for example when the app starts (see section 2).
  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking.
  • Paid items are paid through Apple or Google only. The developer never receives card or payment details.

1. Data stored only on your device

The app keeps the following on your device and never sends it to the developer:

  • Server list: the server addresses and names you add, and per-server settings
  • App settings: alert keywords, filter settings, the list of players you muted (blocked) and your other app settings
  • Chat history: recent messages per server (up to 2,000 lines per server; older lines are deleted automatically)
  • Account details: your Minecraft player name and player UUID, your skin's URL, your Xbox user hash and the public part of the chat signing key with its signature
  • Sign-in tokens and the chat signing key, kept in the operating system's secure storage (iOS Keychain, Android Keystore-backed encrypted storage) and limited to this device
  • Passwords you enter for a server's login plugin (auto login), kept in the operating system's secure storage on this device only
  • The messages and commands you set to send automatically (on connect, repeating), with the server settings
  • Downloaded game language files (used to display translated chat messages)
  • Whether you bought Pro (a saved value and the time it was last updated, so the features work without internet). No card details or order history are stored

The app never sees or stores your Microsoft account password. You sign in on Microsoft's own sign-in page.

2. Data the app sends, only for the app to work and for the features you use

Some of these connections happen automatically, without a tap: (1) when the app reconnects, when a server moves you, or when the server list appears and the app checks the status of your saved servers; (2) and (4) when one of those connections needs them; and (3) and (5) when the app starts.

(1) Minecraft servers you choose, and servers they move you to

When you connect, the app sends that server your player name, player UUID, the chat messages and commands you send, standard game-client settings (such as language and view distance) and the public key used for chat signing. While you type a command, the text typed so far is sent to that server to get completions, as in the game, even if you never send it. When you turn them on, it also sends your login-plugin password (auto login; only to the server address you saved it for), the messages and commands you set to send automatically, and an arm swing about once a minute (AFK-kick prevention). Servers that use a login plugin usually do not encrypt the connection, so the login-plugin password travels to that server unencrypted; use a password you use nowhere else. Checking a server's status (player count) also connects to that server; the app does this by itself when the server list appears.

The app can also connect without a tap. It rejoins when you return to it after your phone paused it. With Pro, auto reconnect is on by default and tries up to 5 times when a connection drops; rejoining after a kick happens only if you turn that on. On Android it stays connected in the background by default, with an ongoing notification, until you disconnect or turn this off in Settings.

A server can move you to another server (a Minecraft feature called transfer). The app follows automatically and shows a line in the chat. The new server receives the same player data and any cookies the first server stored, and if it is an online-mode server, the join in (2) happens for it too. It never receives your saved login-plugin password or your automatic messages.

These servers are run by third parties unrelated to the developer, under their own policies. Server operators can see your IP address and chat. Some servers (offline-mode servers) do not encrypt traffic.

(2) Microsoft, Xbox and Mojang (sign-in, and joining online-mode servers)

Joining a server requires signing in with a Microsoft account that owns Minecraft: Java Edition. When you sign in, and again when the app renews your sign-in before connecting, the app talks directly to these services: it sends your sign-in tokens and receives new tokens, your Minecraft profile, the chat signing key and the list of Minecraft products on the account (used only to check that it owns Java Edition, and not stored):

login.microsoftonline.com, user.auth.xboxlive.com, xsts.auth.xboxlive.com, api.minecraftservices.com, sessionserver.mojang.com

When you join an online-mode server, the app also sends your Minecraft access token, player UUID and a server hash to sessionserver.mojang.com.

Microsoft's privacy statement applies: https://privacy.microsoft.com

(3) Mojang public file servers

To display translated chat, the app downloads game language files from piston-meta.mojang.com and resources.download.minecraft.net when it starts: on first launch, before you have accepted the terms, then about once a week, and when you switch language. No personal data is sent (your IP address is visible to the server, as with any internet request).

With the bubbles chat style and "Show player faces" on, the app downloads the player skin images a server names from textures.minecraft.net. No personal data is sent here either (your IP address is visible). The skin ids and face images of players shown in chat stay on this device; turning the option off deletes them, and deleting a server or its chat history deletes that server's entries.

(4) Server address lookup (DNS-over-HTTPS)

Whenever the app connects to, or refreshes the status of, a server named by domain name without a port (or with the default port 25565), including a server another server moves you to, it looks up the server's SRV record by sending the domain name to Cloudflare's public DNS-over-HTTPS service (cloudflare-dns.com), or to Google's (dns.google) if Cloudflare does not answer. These services see your IP address and the server address looked up. Addresses given as an IP address or with a port other than 25565 are not looked up.

(5) Apple and Google (in-app purchases)

Purchases and Restore purchases are handled by the App Store or Google Play on your device. Payment details and order history are processed by Apple or Google under their own privacy policies. When the app starts, when you open the Purchases tab and when you return to the app, the app asks the store for product prices and your purchase status, even if you never buy anything. The developer can see sales totals and order information in the store consoles, but the app itself sends the developer nothing. No separate server or third-party payment service is used to check purchases.

(6) Report e-mails (only when you send one yourself)

When you report a message or a player, the app prefills an e-mail to msgcraft1@gmail.com in your mail app, or copies the same text for you. It contains the server's name and address, the reported player's name and UUID, the time and the message. The app does not send it by itself; it reaches the developer only if you review and send it. If you do, the developer receives that content and your e-mail address, and uses them to handle the report. When needed, the developer passes on to the server's operators what they need to act on it (the server, the reported player, the time and the message), not your e-mail address. The e-mail is stored in the developer's Gmail mailbox (Google, outside Korea); section 4 says how long.

The services in (2) to (5) are located outside Korea, mainly in the United States. The developer does not transfer your data to them; your device connects to them directly when the app needs them, in some cases automatically as described above.

The outside services the developer chose are the DNS services in (4) and the Gmail mailbox in (6). For these the developer relies on the providers' published privacy commitments for protection equal to this policy's: Cloudflare (https://developers.cloudflare.com/1.1.1.1/privacy/public-dns-resolver/), Google Public DNS (https://developers.google.com/speed/public-dns/privacy) and Google (https://policies.google.com/privacy). The other services above are the game's and the stores' own services, under their own policies. Operators of Minecraft servers are outside this commitment: the developer does not choose them and cannot promise how they protect your data.

3. What we do not collect

  • No advertising, analytics, tracking or crash-reporting tools.
  • No access to location, contacts, photos, microphone or camera.
  • The developer has no servers, so the developer cannot see your chat or account data. The only exception is the content of a report e-mail you send yourself (see 2(6)).
  • Notifications are local notifications created on your device (no push notification server).
  • App stores (Google Play, App Store) may collect data such as download statistics and purchases under their own policies.

4. Retention and deletion

  • Data stays on your device until you delete it. Chat history keeps only the latest 2,000 lines per server.
  • Removing an account in the app's settings deletes that account's tokens and signing key from the device.
  • You can clear a server's chat history from the chat screen menu; deleting a server also deletes its chat history, and removing an account deletes its sign-in data.
  • The purchase record itself stays with your Apple or Google account; after reinstalling, use Restore purchases.
  • Uninstalling the app deletes its data. On iPhone, however, the system may keep Microsoft sign-in data stored in the Keychain (secure storage) after the app is deleted; the app erases such leftovers the next time it is installed and opened. To erase them right away, remove the account in the app before uninstalling it.
  • You can revoke the app's access to your Microsoft account at any time at https://account.live.com/consent/Manage
  • Deleting a server or an account, or changing a server's address, deletes the login-plugin passwords saved for it. The same iPhone Keychain note applies: leftovers are erased the next time the app is opened.
  • You can share a server's chat log with another app through the system share sheet; the app does not send it anywhere by itself. A shared chat log contains the names and messages of other players in that server's chat (players you muted are left out); share it only where appropriate.
  • Chat you already sent to a Minecraft server may be kept by that server's operators; the developer cannot delete it. Please contact the server's operators.
  • A report e-mail you sent (see 2(6)) stays in the developer's Gmail mailbox for one year after the report is handled, then is deleted from the mailbox. You can ask for it to be deleted sooner at msgcraft1@gmail.com. What was already passed to a server's operators is held by them; please contact them about it.

5. Children

The app is not directed at children under 13. In the Republic of Korea, users under 14 need the consent of a legal guardian.

6. Security

Sign-in tokens are kept in the operating system's secure storage. Traffic to Microsoft, Xbox and Mojang uses HTTPS, and traffic to online-mode servers uses the encryption of the Minecraft protocol. The app does not write sign-in tokens or server passwords to its logs or chat history.

7. Your rights

The data the app keeps stays on your device, so you can view, correct and delete it directly in the app. A report e-mail you sent is the exception: to view, correct or delete the copy the developer holds, write to the address below. A legal guardian of a user under 14 can exercise these rights on the child's behalf, in the app on the device or by e-mail to the address below. Purchase and order records are managed by Apple or Google and cannot be deleted in the app; make such requests through your account with that company or its support. For questions, including about the order information the developer can see in the store consoles, contact us at the address below.

(4) 서버 주소 조회(DNS-over-HTTPS)

포트 없이(또는 기본 포트 25565를 적어) 도메인 이름으로 지정한 서버(다른 서버가 옮겨 보내는 서버 포함)에 접속하거나 서버 목록에서 상태를 확인할 때마다, 서버 주소(도메인 이름)의 SRV 레코드를 Cloudflare(cloudflare-dns.com)의 공개 DNS-over-HTTPS 서비스에 보내 조회합니다(응답이 없으면 Google(dns.google)). 이때 이 서비스들은 여러분의 IP 주소와 조회한 서버 주소를 보게 됩니다. IP 주소나 25565가 아닌 포트를 적은 주소는 조회하지 않습니다.
