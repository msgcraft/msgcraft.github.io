MsgCraft Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 12, 2026
Developer: MsgCraft developer (an individual)
Contact: msgcraft1@gmail.com

MsgCraft ("the app") is an unofficial app for reading and sending chat on Minecraft: Java Edition servers.
NOT AN OFFICIAL MINECRAFT PRODUCT. NOT APPROVED BY OR ASSOCIATED WITH MOJANG OR MICROSOFT.

Summary

  • The developer runs no servers, and the app sends the developer nothing. If you buy a paid item, the developer can see only the sales totals and order information that Apple or Google shows in its store console (see 2(5)). If you send a report or support e-mail yourself, the developer receives that e-mail (see 2(6)). If you open a ticket on the MsgCraft Discord support server, the developer receives what you post there (see 2(7)).
  • The app stores its data only on your device (it is not included in Android's Google Drive backup; on iPhone it can be part of your own iCloud or computer backup of the device).
  • The app connects directly to the Minecraft servers you choose and any server they move you to (see 2(1)), to the Microsoft, Xbox and Mojang services needed for sign-in, to Mojang's file servers for language files, to Mojang's profile services when you look up a player or faces are shown, to Cloudflare or Google to look up server addresses, to the App Store or Google Play, and to imgur, YouTube and Gyazo for chat link previews (other sites only when you tap). Some of these connections happen automatically, for example when the app starts (see section 2).
  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking.
  • Paid items are paid through Apple or Google only. The developer never receives card or payment details.

1. Data stored only on your device

The app keeps the following on your device and never sends it to the developer:

  • Server list: the server addresses and names you add, and per-server settings
  • App settings: alert keywords, filter settings, the list of players you muted (blocked) and your other app settings
  • Chat history: recent messages per server (up to 2,000 lines per server; older lines are deleted automatically)
  • Account details: your Minecraft player name and player UUID, your skin's URL, your Xbox user hash and the public part of the chat signing key with its signature
  • Sign-in tokens and the chat signing key, kept in the operating system's secure storage (iOS Keychain, Android Keystore-backed encrypted storage) and limited to this device
  • Passwords you enter for a server's login plugin (auto login), kept in the operating system's secure storage on this device only
  • The messages and commands you set to send automatically (on connect, repeating), with the server settings
  • The commands you save as quick-command buttons on the chat screen, with the server settings
  • The players you found with "Find a player" (the last 10): their Minecraft name, UUID and when you found them
  • Your friends list (up to 100): the Minecraft name, the UUID (for a Mojang account) and the date added of each player you starred on a player card. The list itself is never sent to the developer or anyone else (with "Show player faces" on, a friend's UUID is used to fetch their face from Mojang's public profile, see 2(3)). "Tell me when a friend joins" only looks at the player list of a server you are already connected to in the app and makes the alert on the device; when friends were online is never stored
  • Downloaded game language files (used to display translated chat messages)
  • Whether you bought Pro (a saved value and the time it was last updated, so the features work without internet). No card details or order history are stored
  • If you turned Pro on with a MsgCraft promo code (Android): that code, the Minecraft player UUID it matches and when you entered it. The app checks the code on the device and sends it nowhere
  • Pictures downloaded for chat link previews may stay for a while in the operating system's image cache (see 2(8))

The app never sees or stores your Microsoft account password. You sign in on Microsoft's own sign-in page.

2. Data the app sends, only for the app to work and for the features you use

Some of these connections happen automatically, without a tap: (1) when the app reconnects, when a server moves you, when the server list appears and the app checks the status of your saved servers, or when you open a server's info page and its status is missing or more than a minute old; (2) and (4) when one of those connections needs them; (3) when the app starts and, with "Show player faces" on, whenever faces appear on screen (for example in chat, the player list, the friends and blocked lists, player cards, the recent lookups in the Skins tab ("Find a player"), or the server list with "Show who is online in the server list" on); (5) when the app starts; and (8), with "Link previews" on, when a chat line with an imgur, YouTube or Gyazo link appears on screen.

(1) Minecraft servers you choose, and servers they move you to

When you connect, the app sends that server your player name, player UUID, the chat messages and commands you send, standard game-client settings (such as language, view distance and whether your name may appear in other people's server lists) and the public key used for chat signing. While you type a command, the text typed so far is sent to that server to get completions, as in the game, even if you never send it. When you turn them on, it also sends your login-plugin password (auto login; only to the server address you saved it for), the messages and commands you set to send automatically, and an arm swing about once a minute (AFK-kick prevention). Servers that use a login plugin usually do not encrypt the connection, so the login-plugin password travels to that server unencrypted; use a password you use nowhere else. Checking a server's status (player count) also connects to that server; the app does this by itself when the server list appears, and when you open a server's info page and its status is missing or more than a minute old. When a server's status reply names some of the players online, the app keeps those names in memory only, to show them in the server list and on the server info page, and never saves them (they are gone when the app closes).

The app can also connect without a tap. It rejoins when you return to it after your phone paused it. With Pro, auto reconnect is on by default and tries up to 5 times when a connection drops; rejoining after a kick happens only if you turn that on. On Android it stays connected in the background by default, with an ongoing notification, until you disconnect or turn this off in Settings.

A server can move you to another server (a Minecraft feature called transfer). The app follows automatically and shows a line in the chat. The new server receives the same player data and any cookies the first server stored, and if it is an online-mode server, the join in (2) happens for it too. It never receives your saved login-plugin password or your automatic messages.

These servers are run by third parties unrelated to the developer, under their own policies. Server operators can see your IP address and chat. Some servers (offline-mode servers) do not encrypt traffic.

(2) Microsoft, Xbox and Mojang (sign-in, and joining online-mode servers)

Joining a server requires signing in with a Microsoft account that owns Minecraft: Java Edition. When you sign in, and again when the app renews your sign-in before connecting, the app talks directly to these services: it sends your sign-in tokens and receives new tokens, your Minecraft profile, the chat signing key and the list of Minecraft products on the account (used only to check that it owns Java Edition, and not stored):

login.microsoftonline.com, user.auth.xboxlive.com, xsts.auth.xboxlive.com, api.minecraftservices.com, sessionserver.mojang.com

When you join an online-mode server, the app also sends your Minecraft access token, player UUID and a server hash to sessionserver.mojang.com.

Microsoft's privacy statement applies: https://privacy.microsoft.com

(3) Mojang public file servers and player profiles

To display translated chat, the app downloads game language files from piston-meta.mojang.com and resources.download.minecraft.net when it starts: on first launch, before you have accepted the terms, then about once a week, and when you switch language. No personal data is sent (your IP address is visible to the server, as with any internet request).

With "Show player faces" on (the default), the app downloads the player skin images a server names, and your accounts' own skin images, from textures.minecraft.net and shows them in chat bubbles, the player list, the account list and player cards. When you tap "View in 3D" on a player card, and when you find a player (or tap a recent lookup) in "Find a player" (below), it downloads that player's skin and cape images from the same place and shows them in 3D. For players whose skin it does not know (such as muted players, friends and players found with "Find a player") it asks sessionserver.mojang.com for that player's public profile by their UUID (no token is sent) to find the skin (and, for the 3D view, the arm model and the cape). It never asks this for UUIDs made by servers without Mojang sign-in (version 3). When you long-press a server in the server list or tap the player count on a server's info page to see who is online, turn on "Show who is online in the server list" or open a player card, it finds that player's face the same way. No personal data is sent here either (your IP address is visible). The skin ids and face images of players shown stay on this device (faces of players seen in the server list or on a server's info page while the app is not connected to that server, faces in the friends list, faces a player card found by the public profile, and the skin and cape images downloaded for the 3D view are kept in memory only, never saved; while the app is connected to that server, the list of who is online shows that server's own player list, and those faces are kept like the player list's); turning the option off deletes them, and deleting a server or its chat history deletes that server's entries.

When you tap Find in "Find a player" (the Skins tab in the bottom bar; it also opens from "Look up at Mojang" in a player list search and from the Friends screen), the app sends the player name you typed to api.minecraftservices.com (or, if that cannot be reached or answers with a server error, to api.mojang.com), or the UUID you typed to sessionserver.mojang.com, and receives that player's public profile (name and UUID). It sends this only when you tap, never while you type, and sends no token or account details of yours (your IP address is visible). It never sends UUIDs made by servers without Mojang sign-in (version 3). The last 10 players found (name, UUID and when) are stored on this device only, until you tap "Clear history". With "Show player faces" on, the player found (or a recent lookup you tap) is shown in 3D at once, without tapping "View in 3D", and their skin and cape images are downloaded the same way as above. The faces of the player found and of the recent lookups, and the images downloaded for the 3D view, are kept in memory only (with "Show player faces" on, the recent lookups' faces are asked for without a tap when the screen opens).

(4) Server address lookup (DNS-over-HTTPS)

Whenever the app connects to, or refreshes the status of, a server named by domain name without a port (or with the default port 25565), including a server another server moves you to, it looks up the server's SRV record by sending the domain name to Cloudflare's public DNS-over-HTTPS service (cloudflare-dns.com), or to Google's (dns.google) if Cloudflare does not answer. These services see your IP address and the server address looked up. Addresses given as an IP address or with a port other than 25565 are not looked up.

(5) Apple and Google (in-app purchases)

Purchases and Restore purchases are handled by the App Store or Google Play on your device. Payment details and order history are processed by Apple or Google under their own privacy policies. When the app starts, when you open the Purchases tab and when you return to the app, the app asks the store for product prices and your purchase status, even if you never buy anything. The developer can see sales totals and order information in the store consoles, but the app itself sends the developer nothing. No separate server or third-party payment service is used to check purchases.

(6) Report and support e-mails (only when you send one yourself)

When you report a message or a player, the app prefills an e-mail to msgcraft1@gmail.com in your mail app, or copies the same text for you. It contains the server's name and address, the reported player's name and UUID, the time and the message (with the previewed link when the message text does not show it), with a space for details you add yourself. Tapping the support address on the app's About screen opens a blank e-mail to the same address in your mail app, and you write the content. The app does not send either by itself; each reaches the developer only if you review and send it. If you do, the developer receives that content, your e-mail address and your sender name (the name set on your mail account), and uses them to handle the report or question. When needed, the developer passes on to the server's operators what they need to act on a report (the server, the reported player, the time and the message), not your e-mail address or sender name. The e-mail is stored in the developer's Gmail mailbox (Google, outside Korea); section 4 says how long.

(7) Discord support (only when you open a ticket yourself)

Tapping "Ask on Discord" on the app's About screen opens the invite link to the MsgCraft Discord support server in the Discord app or your browser. The app only opens the link: it adds none of your data to the link and sends nothing to Discord. Discord is optional; you can send the same request to msgcraft1@gmail.com instead, and not using Discord has no effect on the app.

When you choose question, suggestion or report on that server, a private channel opens that only you and the developer (staff) can see. The developer receives your Discord username, display name, avatar and user ID, and the messages, attachments and form answers you post in that channel, with the times the ticket was opened and closed, and uses them to handle your request. In that channel you and the staff can see each other's Discord profiles, and other people who joined the server can see your name and avatar in the server's member list. Never send passwords or sign-in codes.

This data is sent over the internet to the following services when you post it, where it is stored or processed. Section 4 says how long the developer keeps it.

  • Discord Inc. (United States and other countries where Discord operates, https://discord.com/privacy): all of the data above. It stores the ticket channel, the transcript files and the log channel's messages until the developer deletes them.
  • Ticket Tool (its operator and server location are not published; its terms are governed by the law of Missouri, USA; support@tickettool.xyz): your Discord username and user ID, your form answers and the ticket's messages. It opens and closes ticket channels, and reads the messages to make transcript files, which it posts to the Discord server above. Ticket Tool states that it keeps no copies of transcripts, but its own privacy policy (https://tickettool.xyz/privacy-policy) sets no fixed period for how long it keeps other data.

Your Discord account itself is handled by Discord under its own privacy policy, and Discord's age requirements apply.

(8) Chat link previews (while Settings > Chat > "Link previews" is on, the default)

When a chat line has a picture or video link, the app shows a small preview under it. For imgur (i.imgur.com), YouTube (video thumbnails from i.ytimg.com) and Gyazo (i.gyazo.com) links, it downloads the picture or thumbnail from that site as soon as the line appears on screen, without a tap. Picture links on any other site are downloaded from that site only when you tap "Load picture". While the chat filter is on, every picture waits for a tap. Tapping a picture preview shows it full screen in the app (a larger picture is downloaded from the same site); videos never play in the app: a tap opens the link in your browser or the YouTube app, after asking.

The site sees your IP address and the address of the picture downloaded, as with any internet request. The app sends no other personal data and no sign-in data. A site run by whoever posted the link could see your IP address, which is why sites other than imgur, YouTube and Gyazo load only when you tap. Links that are not encrypted (http), that use an IP address or a local network name, and links from players you blocked are never previewed. Downloaded pictures may stay for a while in the operating system's image cache and are deleted with the app.

The services in (2) to (5) and (8) are located outside Korea, mainly in the United States. The developer does not transfer your data to them; your device connects to them directly when the app needs them, in some cases automatically as described above.

Among the outside services the developer chose, for the DNS services in (4) and the Gmail mailbox in (6) the developer relies on the providers' published privacy commitments for protection equal to this policy's: Cloudflare (https://developers.cloudflare.com/1.1.1.1/privacy/public-dns-resolver/), Google Public DNS (https://developers.google.com/speed/public-dns/privacy) and Google (https://policies.google.com/privacy). The other services above are the game's and the stores' own services, under their own policies. Operators of Minecraft servers are outside this commitment: the developer does not choose them and cannot promise how they protect your data.

imgur, YouTube and Gyazo in (8) are where links posted in the chat point, under their own privacy policies: imgur (https://imgur.com/privacy), YouTube (Google, https://policies.google.com/privacy) and Gyazo (run by Helpfeel, https://corp.helpfeel.com/en/privacy). Other picture sites are chosen by whoever posted the link, so the developer cannot promise how they protect your data.

Discord and Ticket Tool in (7) are also services the developer chose, but without that commitment. The developer cannot sign a separate agreement with them, so their published terms and privacy policies apply (https://discord.com/privacy, https://tickettool.xyz/privacy-policy). If that concerns you, please write by e-mail instead of using Discord.

3. What we do not collect

  • No advertising, analytics, tracking or crash-reporting tools.
  • No access to location, contacts, photos, microphone or camera.
  • The developer has no servers, so the developer cannot see your chat or account data. The only exceptions are a report or support e-mail you send yourself and a Discord support ticket you open yourself (see 2(6) and 2(7)).
  • Notifications are local notifications created on your device (no push notification server).
  • App stores (Google Play, App Store) may collect data such as download statistics and purchases under their own policies.

4. Retention and deletion

  • Data stays on your device until you delete it. Chat history keeps only the latest 2,000 lines per server.
  • The recent lookups of "Find a player" (the last 10) can be deleted with "Clear history" in the Skins tab in the bottom bar.
  • Friends can be removed one by one in Settings > Players > Friends.
  • Removing an account in the app's settings deletes that account's tokens and signing key from the device.
  • You can clear a server's chat history from the chat screen menu; deleting a server also deletes its chat history, and removing an account deletes its sign-in data.
  • The purchase record itself stays with your Apple or Google account; after reinstalling, use Restore purchases.
  • Uninstalling the app deletes its data. On iPhone, however, the system may keep Microsoft sign-in data stored in the Keychain (secure storage) after the app is deleted; the app erases such leftovers the next time it is installed and opened. To erase them right away, remove the account in the app before uninstalling it.
  • You can revoke the app's access to your Microsoft account at any time at https://account.live.com/consent/Manage
  • Deleting a server or an account, or changing a server's address, deletes the login-plugin passwords saved for it. The same iPhone Keychain note applies: leftovers are erased the next time the app is opened.
  • You can share a server's chat log with another app through the system share sheet; the app does not send it anywhere by itself. A shared chat log contains the names and messages of other players in that server's chat (players you muted are left out); share it only where appropriate.
  • You can send a saved server's name and address, with a link that adds it in the app (https://msgcraft.github.io/add/), to another app through the system share sheet ("Share" in the server card's menu or on the server info page). Nothing else goes in (no account or other settings), and the app does not send it anywhere by itself. The link's web page reads the name and address from the part of the link after "#" only inside the browser of whoever opens it, and neither stores nor sends them (the part after "#" is never sent to the web server).
  • Pictures downloaded for chat link previews stay only in the operating system's image cache for a while and are deleted with the app. Turning off Settings > Chat > "Link previews" stops loading them.
  • Chat you already sent to a Minecraft server may be kept by that server's operators; the developer cannot delete it. Please contact the server's operators.
  • A report or support e-mail you sent (see 2(6)) stays in the developer's Gmail mailbox for one year after it is handled, then is deleted from the mailbox. You can ask for it to be deleted sooner at msgcraft1@gmail.com. What was already passed to a server's operators is held by them; please contact them about it.
  • A Discord support ticket (see 2(7)) is deleted when it has been handled. The transcript file and log messages kept in staff-only channels are kept for one year after the ticket is handled, then deleted. You can ask for earlier deletion in the ticket or at msgcraft1@gmail.com. Your Discord account and messages you post elsewhere are managed by Discord: delete them in Discord or ask Discord.

5. Children

The app is not directed at children under 13. In the Republic of Korea, users under 14 need the consent of a legal guardian.

6. Security

Sign-in tokens are kept in the operating system's secure storage. Traffic to Microsoft, Xbox and Mojang uses HTTPS, and traffic to online-mode servers uses the encryption of the Minecraft protocol. The app does not write sign-in tokens or server passwords to its logs or chat history. Discord ticket channels are visible only to the person who opened them and staff, and transcripts and the log channel to staff only (the Ticket Tool bot has only the permissions it needs to work); staff Discord accounts use two-factor authentication, and transcripts are not saved anywhere else, such as Google Drive.

7. Your rights

The data the app keeps stays on your device, so you can view, correct and delete it directly in the app. A report or support e-mail you sent and a Discord support ticket you opened are the exceptions: to view, correct or delete the copy the developer holds, write to the address below (or, for a Discord ticket, ask in the ticket). A legal guardian of a user under 14 can exercise these rights on the child's behalf, in the app on the device or by e-mail to the address below. Purchase and order records are managed by Apple or Google and cannot be deleted in the app; make such requests through your account with that company or its support. For questions, including about the order information the developer can see in the store consoles, contact us at the address below.
